# Human-Simple Frontstage

## Mission

Convert backstage complexity into frontstage clarity.

Treat **frontstage** as every word the audience sees or hears: headings, buttons, forms, menus, explanations, errors, onboarding, sales copy, educational scripts, captions, and notices.

Do not merely shorten text. Preserve the intended meaning, truth, useful distinction, and next action.

> ساده‌سازی یعنی همان مفهوم، با بار ذهنی کمتر.
>
> ساده‌سازی یعنی احترام به وقت و ذهن مخاطب؛ نه بچگانه‌کردن او.

## Instruction precedence

Apply this order whenever instructions conflict:

1. Verified facts, real product behavior, legal and safety requirements
2. Locked project documents and explicit source-of-truth files
3. The user’s requested audience, tone, format, and constraints
4. This skill

Never use this skill to overwrite a factual, legal, brand, or product decision. If the source material does not support a claim, do not invent it. Mark the gap or ask for the missing fact.

## Core workflow

### 1. Identify the frontstage job

Determine:

- Who is reading or listening?
- What do they already know?
- What single thing must they understand?
- What should they feel?
- What should they do next?
- Where will the text appear: mobile hero, button, form, error, privacy notice, lesson, story, or another surface?

Do not write until the job is clear.

### 2. Lock the meaning

Before rewriting, silently separate:

- **Must keep:** facts, promise boundaries, conditions, prices, dates, names, legal meaning, and required actions
- **May simplify:** terminology, sentence structure, repetition, abstract phrasing, and internal process language
- **Must verify:** claims, features, metrics, availability, guarantees, or functionality not proven by the supplied source

Never create clarity by deleting an important condition.

### 3. Choose the right register

Use the project’s own voice when available. Otherwise choose one of these:

- **Human-neutral:** clear, direct, calm; default for most UI
- **Warm-personal:** conversational and close; suitable for founder brands and education
- **Trust-formal:** simple but restrained; suitable for payment, privacy, medical, legal, and high-stakes copy
- **Social-native:** natural spoken Persian; suitable for stories, reels, and community messages
- **Premium-minimal:** few words, high specificity, no hype; suitable for luxury or authority-led brands

Do not confuse warmth with slang. Do not confuse authority with bureaucratic language.

### 4. Simplify the concept, not the label

Do not translate internal terminology word for word. Explain what it means for the user.

Examples:

- `Diagnostic` → «ببین از کجا شروع کنی» or «چند سؤال برای شناخت تو»
- `Flagship program` → «برنامه اصلی»
- `Activation` → «اولین قدمت رو بردار»
- `Lead` → «درخواست» or «اطلاعات تماس»
- `Workflow` → «مراحل کار» or «روال کار»

Read `references/term-map-fa.md` when the source contains product, marketing, AI, analytics, or UX jargon.

### 5. Rewrite with concrete language

Prefer:

- verbs over abstract nouns
- concrete outcomes over feature labels
- one idea per sentence
- familiar words over literal translations
- visible actions over vague promises
- short, useful context over slogans

Ask: «این جمله دقیقاً چه تصویر یا کاری در ذهن مخاطب می‌سازه؟»

If the answer is vague, rewrite it.

### 6. Make the next action obvious

The user should know what happens after reading or tapping.

Prefer:

- «رایگان شروع کن»
- «آموزش رو ببین»
- «درخواست مشاوره»
- «شماره‌ت رو یه بار چک کن»

Avoid vague labels when a specific action is available:

- «بیشتر بدانید»
- «کلیک کنید»
- «ارسال»
- «ورود به فرایند»

Generic labels are acceptable only when the surrounding UI already makes the action unmistakable.

### 7. Run the quality gates

Every final string must pass:

1. **Truth gate:** Is every claim accurate and supported?
2. **Meaning gate:** Did the important meaning survive?
3. **Three-second gate:** Can the intended audience understand it immediately?
4. **Class-five gate:** Does it work without specialist knowledge?
5. **Speech gate:** Could a real person naturally say it?
6. **Action gate:** Is the next step clear where an action is needed?
7. **Dignity gate:** Is it simple without sounding childish, cheap, or patronizing?
8. **Mobile gate:** Can it be scanned on a small screen without becoming a wall of text?
9. **Brand gate:** Does it sound like this project rather than a generic AI writer?

If any mandatory gate fails, rewrite before presenting the result. Read `references/quality-gates.md` for the full test.

## Surface-specific rules

### Headlines and hero copy

- Communicate one central idea.
- Lead with the audience’s situation or desired result, not an internal category.
- Keep mobile line length and hierarchy in mind.
- Avoid stacking several promises in one headline.
- Replace generic claims with a specific point of view.

Bad:

«هوش مصنوعی را فقط یاد نگیر؛ یاد بگیر که استفاده‌اش کنی.»

Stronger when it matches the brand promise:

«قرار نیست همه‌ی هوش مصنوعی رو یاد بگیری؛ فقط چیزی که به درد تو می‌خوره.»

### Buttons and links

- Use a clear verb or outcome.
- Describe the immediate next step.
- Keep the label short enough for mobile.
- Do not promise a result the destination cannot deliver.

### Forms

- Ask questions like a helpful person, not an office form.
- Explain why sensitive or unusual information is needed.
- Put the easiest questions first unless the flow requires qualification first.
- Keep optional fields visibly optional.
- Use examples only when they genuinely reduce confusion.

### Errors

State:

1. what went wrong
2. what the user can do now

Avoid blame, jokes, codes, and vague failure language.

Bad:

«INVALID_INPUT_ERROR»

Better:

«شماره‌ت کامل نیست. یه بار چکش کن.»

### Success messages

Confirm what happened, then show the next step.

Bad:

«عملیات با موفقیت انجام شد!»

Better:

«درخواستت ثبت شد. بعد از بررسی باهات تماس می‌گیریم.»

### Onboarding and multi-step flows

- Show one decision at a time.
- Tell the user where they are only when it helps.
- Use progress language that feels human.
- Do not expose internal system stages.

### Privacy, consent, legal, medical, and financial copy

- Keep the legal or safety meaning intact.
- Use plain language, but do not remove conditions, uncertainty, or risk.
- Never claim compliance without verification.
- Separate the simple summary from the complete legal text when both are required.

### Educational explanations

Use this sequence when helpful:

1. familiar situation
2. simple explanation
3. concrete example or analogy
4. one action the learner can try

Do not overload one explanation with every exception. Mention the exception that changes the learner’s decision; move advanced detail to the next layer.

## Persian writing rules

- Write natural Persian, not translated English syntax.
- Use نیم‌فاصله consistently where it improves readability.
- Prefer short active sentences.
- Preserve exact names of tools, brands, people, prices, dates, and technical identifiers.
- Keep URLs, code, event names, and file paths unchanged unless the task explicitly asks to rename them.
- Avoid excessive Arabic-heavy, administrative, and academic wording when a familiar Persian alternative exists.
- Avoid emoji, exclamation marks, and decorative punctuation unless the brief clearly needs a social or playful tone.
- Do not force every technical word into Persian if the audience already knows the original term. Explain unfamiliar meaning instead.
- Read `references/persian-style.md` for mixed Persian/English, digits, punctuation, and tone details.

## Anti-patterns

Never default to:

- literal translation of internal terminology
- AI-copy clichés such as «تحول»، «انقلاب»، «قدرت را آزاد کن»، «تجربه‌ای بی‌نظیر»، or «راهکار جامع»
- feature dumps without a user outcome
- vague calls to action
- fake urgency or unsupported certainty
- childish oversimplification
- excessive slang that damages trust
- long formal sentences that hide the action
- repeating one brand word until it becomes a buzzword
- inventing a feature because the copy sounds better with it

## Output modes

Follow the user’s requested format exactly.

### Final-only mode

When the user says «فقط متن»، «فقط جایگزین»، «لیست اسم فقط»، or otherwise asks for no explanation, output only the final copy.

### Exact-replacement mode

For implementation-ready editing, use:

```text
متن فعلی:
«...»

جایگزین دقیق:
«...»
```

Do not add new sections unless asked.

### Audit mode

Use a compact structure:

- مشکل
- اثر روی مخاطب
- جایگزین دقیق

### Bulk UI mode

Use a table or numbered list with:

- surface/key
- current copy
- final copy
- implementation note only when needed

### Variant mode

Provide variants only when the user asks or a genuine decision remains. Make each variant meaningfully different; do not create five cosmetic rewrites.

Read `references/output-formats.md` for exact templates.

## Working inside code or a repository

- Read the project’s source-of-truth documents first.
- Change visible copy only unless the task explicitly includes behavior or layout.
- Preserve IDs, names, routes, form field keys, analytics event names, ARIA relationships, and backend contracts.
- Do not turn unimplemented functionality into a promise.
- Verify that a CTA destination exists before writing copy that implies it works.
- Keep copy changes reviewable and separate from unrelated refactors.
- Read `references/project-integration.md` when working with design, UX-writing, RTL, QA, or repository skills.

## Final self-check

Before delivering, ask silently:

- What does the audience understand now?
- What important meaning could have been lost?
- What do they do next?
- Does this sound human when read aloud?
- Is it simple because it is clear, or merely short?

Then provide the requested output without explaining the entire internal process.
